When Perfect Isn't Enough

I didn't realize I was a perfectionist at first. I thought I was just disciplined.

It started with wanting to become a better version of myself. I'd tell myself that if I lost a little more weight, ate cleaner, worked out harder, or stayed more consistent, I'd finally feel confident. Every goal became another finish line that kept moving further away.

The gym became more than just exercise. It became proof that I was trying hard enough. Missing a workout felt like failing. Eating something outside my plan made me feel guilty, and I'd spend more time thinking about how to make up for it than enjoying the moment. I wasn't listening to my body anymore — I was listening to the voice that kept saying, "You can do better."

It wasn't just about food or fitness. It was about control.

When other parts of life felt overwhelming, controlling my routine made me feel like I had everything together. But the more I chased perfection, the more impossible it became to reach.

Even when I made progress, I barely let myself celebrate it because I was already focused on what still wasn't enough. The hardest part was that from the outside, it probably looked healthy. I was going to the gym, setting goals, trying to improve myself. But inside, I was exhausted from constantly measuring my worth against impossible standards.

I'm still learning that discipline and self-worth aren't the same thing.

Now I'm trying to move my body because it makes me feel strong, not because I need to earn confidence. I'm learning that one meal, one missed workout, or one imperfect day doesn't erase all of my progress.

For so long, I believed perfection would finally make me feel enough.

Now I'm starting to believe that I was enough before I ever started chasing it.
