Opening Up & Trust 7 min read

Letting Myself Be Known

I was afraid of being understood. What if they judge me? What if they think differently of me after I tell them?

Those thoughts ran through my mind constantly whenever I thought about opening up to someone. Keeping my feelings to myself felt like the safest option. I became so used to hiding how I truly felt that opening up to others seemed almost impossible. I told myself I was better off handling everything alone — that I didn't need to rely on anyone else. I had this idea that at the end of the day, all you've got is yourself, and no one is going to save you.

Eventually, I found a kind of peace in not opening up to others. It became familiar. Almost like it was who I was. But deep down, I knew that wasn't what I truly wanted.

A part of me wanted someone I could talk to — someone who would listen without judging me and actually understand what I was going through. I just didn't know how to let someone in. Whenever I got close to someone and felt myself letting them into my life, I got scared. I was afraid that if they truly got to know me, they would judge me, see me differently, and eventually leave.

That began to change when I met my homestay family. When I first met them, I tried so hard to let them into my life, because we were living together. But I could never fully do it — even though they were kind and welcoming, I was still scared. I had spent so much time keeping my feelings to myself that opening up felt uncomfortable and unfamiliar. I genuinely believed I could do everything on my own. I'd tell myself I was fine keeping it all inside, but deep down, I still wanted someone I could truly talk to.

At the time, I had already been struggling with my mental health for a long time. I was carrying a lot of emotions I didn't know how to express, and over time it got harder to deal with everything alone. I started isolating myself, distancing myself from the people who cared about me. I knew my homestay family cared, and I felt sorry for pushing them away when they were only trying to help. But even though I wanted their support deep down, I was still afraid to open up and let them see what I was truly going through. I didn't know how to open up without hiding behind a lie.

As my mental health continued to get worse, my homestay family began to notice something was wrong. They'd ask how I was doing. Sometimes my homestay mom would come up to my room to check on me, see if I was okay, and remind me they were there. At first, I still struggled to tell them what was going on in my head — I wasn't used to talking about my feelings. But they never pressured me, and never made me feel like a burden.

Slowly, because of that, I started opening up about how I was feeling. The first time I finally let it out, I started crying in front of my homestay mom. I was embarrassed, and scared she would judge me. But she just sat beside me and listened. At that moment, for the first time in my life, I started to feel like I didn't have to hide my feelings — that there are people who would be there for me even if they saw my flaws.

"I am safe here."
"I was free."

For the first time, I felt safe enough to be honest about how I was feeling. I didn't have to pretend I was always happy, or that everything was fine. I could finally talk about the things I had kept inside for so long. It felt like a weight had been lifted off my shoulders. I realized how exhausting it had been to carry everything on my own, and how comforting it was to finally talk to someone about how I'd really been feeling.

With their support, and by starting to see a therapist, my mental health got better and better. When I think about it now, I realize how much I was carrying by myself. At the time, I thought keeping everything inside was what made me strong. Now I understand that I didn't always have to handle everything alone. Being able to open up to my homestay family showed me how comforting it can be to have people who genuinely care about you. Looking back, I'm grateful I finally allowed myself to let them in.

Opening up to others can be scary. But letting the right people into your life — and allowing yourself to trust people who care about you — can help you realize you don't have to face everything on your own.
